# Meme-Native Hotspot

Write as a real person who encountered something strange while using the internet, then kept looking until the mechanism became visible. The article should feel like a friend showing evidence, not a brand presenting slides.

Read [references/style-profile.md](references/style-profile.md) before drafting. Read [references/source-notes.md](references/source-notes.md) when selecting samples or updating the method.

## Choose the mode

### Natural hotspot

Use when the strange behavior, meme, platform trend, price, or public event is the subject. Follow the trail from first encounter to explanation without forcing a product solution.

### Sponsored story

Use when a product is in scope. Start from a real user problem or strange encounter that the product genuinely addresses. Disclose the commercial relationship according to publication requirements. Do not invent a crisis, colleague, client, payment, or test result to make the integration feel natural.

In sponsored mode, preserve one honest question the buyer might ask, including a limitation or competing option when relevant. Native integration means the product enters the story for a reason; it does not mean disguising advertising as independent reporting.

## Build the encounter

Collect the raw trail:

- what appeared on the screen or in conversation;
- the writer's first interpretation;
- the next example that made the situation stranger;
- the search, test, purchase, or comparison that followed;
- the mechanism that finally explained the behavior;
- what the writer now thinks differently.

Open at the first abnormal moment. Let readers share the initial misunderstanding before correcting it. Use screenshots, comments, prices, clips, or interface details to escalate the case.

## Control the voice

Write in short conversational beats and allow immediate reactions, but make each reaction respond to a specific fact. Parentheses can carry an aside or second thought. Questions can dramatize confusion. Neither should become constant decoration.

Use nicknames or slang only if they are natural to the intended narrator and clear to the audience. Do not copy signature expressions from a source account. Remove meme language that adds no information, turn, or comic compression.

## Reveal the mechanism

The second half must answer why the strange thing exists. Depending on the topic, explain the incentive, product loop, pricing structure, platform distribution, social psychology, or operational constraint.

Do not let the mechanism section suddenly become a corporate white paper. Keep returning to the original examples and ask whether the explanation accounts for what the writer observed.

For a product, organize features around the user's sequence of need. Show what the writer did and what changed. Do not enumerate every selling point.

## End in character

End with the narrator's updated behavior, a callback to the opening encounter, or one precise unresolved concern. Avoid both inflated industry prophecy and an abrupt coupon-style call to action.

## Quality gate

- The opening contains a believable encounter, not “最近某某发布了”.
- The strangeness escalates through at least two concrete observations.
- Reactions are attached to evidence.
- The article explains a mechanism, not only a meme.
- Sponsored content is disclosed and includes only tested or sourced claims.
- Product features appear in the order a user needs them.
- At least one honest friction, boundary, or comparison survives editing.
- The ending returns to the narrator rather than switching to brand voice.
