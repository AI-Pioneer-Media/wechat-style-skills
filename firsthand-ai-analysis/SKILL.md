# Firsthand AI Analysis

Write as a participant who investigated the event, not as a narrator summarizing press releases. The article should let readers see what happened, understand why it matters, and know which judgment is yours.

Before drafting, read [references/style-profile.md](references/style-profile.md). When reviewing the evidence behind the method or updating the skill, also read [references/source-notes.md](references/source-notes.md).

## Establish the article's contract

Compress the topic into three statements before writing:

- **Old expectation:** what readers reasonably believed before this event.
- **Observed anomaly:** the specific result, action, number, failure, or contradiction that no longer fits.
- **Consequence:** whose cost, workflow, advantage, or risk changes if the anomaly persists.

If there is no observable anomaly, treat the item as news worth summarizing, not automatically as a deep article.

## Build an evidence pack

Separate evidence into three layers:

1. **Primary facts:** official release, paper, repository, filing, product page, or direct statement.
2. **Firsthand evidence:** what the writer actually opened, paid for, entered, waited for, received, retried, compared, or failed to complete.
3. **Independent context:** external tests, history, competing explanations, and limits.

Never fabricate a test, payment, dialogue, timeline, or emotional reaction. If firsthand testing is unavailable, say so and replace “我试了” with a transparent research narrative.

Record friction as evidence: price, queue time, confusing controls, failed prompts, inconsistent output, manual cleanup, and conditions needed to reproduce the result. These details create credibility and often reveal the real product boundary.

## Draft the argument

Open with a concrete event or action. Within the first screen, show the anomaly and explain why the writer chose to investigate it. Do not spend the opening announcing that the topic is important.

State a provisional judgment early enough to create tension, then earn it through evidence. A useful progression is:

> striking event → why I tested it → what happened → what went wrong or changed → mechanism → who is affected → boundary → larger consequence

This is a decision path, not a mandatory heading template. Merge or reorder sections when the material demands it.

Move repeatedly between three scales:

- **screen:** the exact output, interface, error, number, or interaction;
- **mechanism:** why the system behaved that way;
- **world:** what changes for users, companies, or an industry.

Do not remain at “world” scale for more than two consecutive paragraphs without returning to evidence.

## Keep the voice human

Use first person only where the writer made a choice, paid a cost, observed a result, or changed their mind. First person is provenance, not decoration.

Prefer short paragraphs and direct judgments, but vary sentence length. Preserve genuine uncertainty and reversals. Remove report-like padding, mechanical transitions, stacked rhetorical questions, and repeated “不是 A，而是 B” constructions.

Humor should compress an observation or release tension. Do not add slang merely to simulate personality. Do not copy catchphrases, metaphors, anecdotes, or sentence rhythms distinctive to any source author.

## End with changed behavior

Return to the opening test, person, or contradiction. State what the writer or reader should now examine differently: a metric to watch, a workflow to change, a promise to distrust, or a cost that has moved.

Avoid generic declarations that “the industry has entered a new era.” The consequence should be testable.

## Quality gate

Before delivery, confirm that:

- the opening contains an event or observation rather than a topic introduction;
- every strong claim can be labeled fact, firsthand observation, or inference;
- at least one cost, failure, limit, or reversal is visible;
- the judgment appears before the article becomes a feature list;
- each section advances the evidence or changes scale;
- uncertainty is disclosed without draining the article of a position;
- the ending changes how the reader will judge the next similar event;
- removing the first-person sentences would remove evidence, not just personality.
