# Style Profile Schema

Use this schema when creating a style analysis or a new account-specific writing Skill. Omit sections that the available evidence cannot support.

## 1. Positioning

- Core audience and their existing knowledge
- Recurring reader problem or emotional need
- Topics the account chooses and topics it avoids
- Education–entertainment balance

## 2. Title system

- Common title structures
- Source of tension: number, contradiction, identity, consequence, novelty, or controversy
- Typical specificity and length
- Patterns that attract clicks but would betray the account's tone

## 3. Opening hook

- What appears in the first 100–300 Chinese characters
- How quickly the central conflict is named
- Whether the opening uses scene, quotation, result, question, or judgment
- What benefit or unresolved question keeps the reader moving

## 4. Structure

- Narrative-to-analysis ratio
- Section progression
- Placement of the strongest evidence and strongest judgment
- How the article changes scale: person → company → industry → society, or the reverse

## 5. Information cadence

- Average paragraph and sentence length
- Frequency of data, examples, quotations, and conclusions
- How often the article delivers a new implication
- Where it deliberately slows down

## 6. Evidence and credibility

- Preferred source types
- Citation or attribution habits
- Use of firsthand testing, screenshots, interviews, or public documents
- How uncertainty and disputed facts are disclosed

## 7. Voice

- Distance from the reader
- Degree of colloquial language
- Humor, irony, anger, warmth, or restraint
- Common transition logic without preserving signature phrases
- Punctuation and emphasis habits

## 8. Ending

- Summary, escalation, callback, open question, or action
- Whether the ending closes the argument or creates discussion
- Typical emotional aftertaste

## 9. Failure modes

List three to five ways a draft can look superficially similar while missing the actual style, such as copying catchphrases, stacking rhetorical questions, forcing controversy, or using unsupported big claims.

## 10. Operational rules

Convert the strongest findings into 8–15 conditional instructions. For each rule, record:

| Rule | Observable evidence | Intended effect | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conditional writing decision | Repeated signal across samples | Reader or editorial effect | High / Medium / Low |
