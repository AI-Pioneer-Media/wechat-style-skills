# Absurd Rigorous Explainer

Treat a question people normally laugh past as if it deserves a research project. The humor should come from the mismatch between the question's apparent triviality and the method's genuine rigor. The reader must still learn something true.

Read [references/style-profile.md](references/style-profile.md) before drafting. Read [references/source-notes.md](references/source-notes.md) when auditing or extending the method.

## Choose a productive question

Prefer a question that is:

- visible in ordinary life, a viral argument, or a familiar fictional scene;
- specific enough to measure, calculate, simulate, or test;
- initially ridiculous but connected to a real mechanism;
- understandable before the reader sees any formula.

Reject questions that are only wordplay, require invented evidence, or collapse after one dictionary definition.

## Make the premise explicit

Before solving, state what the key words mean and which scenario is being tested. Many viral disputes hide multiple incompatible premises. Separate them instead of arguing over an ambiguous sentence.

List plausible hypotheses. Explain what observation would distinguish them. Use the lightest method capable of answering the question: measurement, back-of-the-envelope calculation, controlled comparison, public dataset, simulation, or expert source.

Never claim to have performed an experiment that was not performed. When using a thought experiment or estimate, label it and show the assumptions.

## Turn the method into a story

A useful progression is:

> familiar scene → unnecessary but irresistible question → definitions → possible answers → method → complication → result → larger insight → dry callback

Do not turn the article into a textbook chapter. Reveal formulas and variables at the moment the investigation needs them. Translate every important quantity into an intuitive comparison.

Let reality resist the method. Measurement noise, an awkward control, an unexpected variable, or a failed first approach creates both credibility and comedy.

## Use humor without sacrificing the answer

Keep the technical nouns accurate. Put the colloquial reaction around the mechanism, not inside the definition. The joke is usually that the writer is taking the problem far more seriously than social convention requires.

Avoid a punchline in every sentence. Alternate deadpan procedure, concrete observation, and occasional emotional reaction. Do not borrow signature phrases, recurring characters, personal anecdotes, or distinctive metaphors from source writers.

## Design evidence and visuals

Use diagrams, annotated screenshots, simple tables, photos of the setup, or calculation cards where they advance the reasoning. A funny reaction image cannot replace the measurement it reacts to.

For each image, know whether it establishes the premise, explains the setup, records a result, or visualizes the conclusion.

## End beyond the gimmick

Answer the original question clearly. Then show the real concept the reader accidentally learned: experimental design, order of magnitude, causal reasoning, geometry, physiology, media literacy, or the value of noticing overlooked phenomena.

The final line may be dry or playful, but should call back to the original scene rather than announce that science is wonderful.

## Quality gate

- The question is funny before the explanation and meaningful after it.
- Definitions and assumptions are visible.
- The method could, in principle, falsify the preferred answer.
- At least one complication or rival explanation is addressed.
- Calculations use units and disclose estimates.
- Images carry evidence or explanation.
- The conclusion answers the literal question before expanding it.
- Removing the jokes would still leave a correct and useful explanation.
